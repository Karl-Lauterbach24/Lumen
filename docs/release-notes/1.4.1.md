## Fixed

- **Blu-ray from a drive, started with its menu: playback stopped for a moment, went on, stopped again.**
  With the disc's menu Lumen plays one continuous stream and keeps only 0.4 seconds of it in memory, so
  that a key in the menu acts at once. An optical drive does not deliver that evenly. Measured on a USB
  drive (ASUS BW-16D1HT, macOS), reading a film the way the player does:
  - the system fetches about 3 MB at a time and the call that asked waits for all of it – 0.2 s, once a
    second, on a good day;
  - now and then it pauses for 0.5 to 0.9 s (seen at the data rate of that disc, 1.9 MB/s);
  - with helper processes of an earlier, crashed run still attached to the drive (see below) it stood
    for 2.6 and for 8.5 s.

  Everything over 0.4 s was a held picture and a gap in the sound. Whether a film ran smoothly depended
  on what the drive happened to do – which is why it sometimes settled and sometimes did not.

  Lumen now **reads ahead of the disc**: a thread of its own reads the same file through a second access
  and keeps about 25 seconds (at least 48 MiB) in front of the place libbluray is reading. That fills the
  file cache of the operating system; libbluray's own reads then come from memory, and when the drive
  pauses, only the reader-ahead waits. libbluray itself reads exactly as before – its position, the
  menus and everything tied to them are untouched, and the 0.4 s stay. The disc library's file access is
  hooked for this (`bd_register_file`), so it holds for the menu, for Blu-ray 3D (both views) and for
  playback without the menu alike.
- **Helper processes left at the drive.** MakeMKV decrypts through a process of its own
  (`makemkvcon guiserver`), started for every disc that is opened. When the program that started it is gone
  without closing the disc – a crash, a forced end – that process stays: it inherits both ends of its own
  connection and never sees it close. Two of them were found on the machine where the stops were
  reported, four and a half hours old. In the measurements there, reading stood for seconds three times
  in three minutes while they ran, and not once in seven minutes after they were ended. Lumen now
  - ends such processes of yours that have no program any more, at its start and before it reads a disc;
  - ends its own when it has to leave without the orderly shutdown (the six-second limit at quitting);
  - shuts down in order when it is ended from outside (logout, a service manager, Ctrl+C) instead of
    dying on the spot.
- **A disc that never finishes opening.** When the AACS library's helper stands in the drive, the call
  that opens the disc never returns, and neither does any later one behind it: "Reading disc structure …"
  stayed for good, and the drive was taken from the system as well. After two minutes Lumen now ends that
  helper – which gave the drive back in two of three cases here –, says that the drive has stopped
  responding, and offers to restart itself (the call inside the library cannot be ended any other way).
- **The quality governor reacted to a stalling disc.** Pictures that are missing because the source
  delivered nothing counted like pictures the machine was too slow for, and scaling quality was taken
  back – on a machine that had no trouble at all – and remembered for two weeks. Seconds in which the
  source stood no longer count. Levels remembered by 1.4.0 and earlier are forgotten once.
- **macOS: the program could end when the player window became visible again.** One crash report of 1.4.0
  shows the render thread failing inside its buffer swap (a write to memory that was no longer there)
  while the main thread was still waiting to bring the OpenGL context up to date after the window was
  exposed. The render thread now learns that the window is visible only after that has happened.
- The drive list is refreshed every three seconds; each time, the structure of an inserted disc was looked
  at again, also while it played. A disc is now looked at once.

## For developers

- `src/DiscReadAhead.*`: the reader-ahead, independent of libbluray (sequential streams per file, up to
  four – an image is one file with picture and second view side by side –, lead by rate, hint for the next
  clip of a playlist). `src/BlurayReadAhead.cpp` hooks libbluray's file access. `tests/readahead_test.cpp`
  runs with the other tests in `tools/test_seq3d.sh`.
- `LUMEN_PERF_LOG=1` prints a line `perf quelle` each second: how long reads of the source stood, mpv's
  buffer, the lead of the reader-ahead, what the disc library consumes and what the drive delivers.
- `LUMEN_NO_READAHEAD=1` switches reading ahead off. `LUMEN_TEST_SLOW_DISC="rate=12e6,stall=2.5,every=9"`
  makes a disc in a folder behave like one in a drive (3 MiB at a time at that rate, a pause every so
  often, what was fetched once comes at once) – for comparing both without a drive.
- `LUMEN_DISC_TIMEOUT=<seconds>` sets the limit for opening a disc, `LUMEN_KEEP_HELPERS=1` leaves helper
  processes without a program alone.

## Checked, and known limits

- **On the drive itself**, outside Lumen, with a program that reads a clip of a pressed Blu-ray the way
  the player does (3 MB/s, data the system had not read before, the leftover helpers still attached in
  both runs): without a second reader 74 reads waited in 75 s, 13 s in sum, the longest 2.6 s; with a
  second reader keeping 64 MiB ahead none waited longer than 1 ms – although the drive stood for 8.5 s
  once during that run.
- **In Lumen**, with a Blu-ray in a folder and the simulated drive (a pause of 2.5 s every 9 s): without
  reading ahead the reads of the source stood for 21.6 s in a minute, the longest 2.6 s, and the picture
  stopped again and again; with it 0.3 s in sum, none over 0.3 s, the picture never stopped. Blu-ray 3D
  (both views), seeking and playback without the menu run with reading ahead as before.
- **Not checked: a pressed disc played from the drive by Lumen itself.** The drive stopped responding
  during the tests – MakeMKV's helper reported `HARDWARE ERROR: TIMEOUT ON LOGICAL UNIT` while opening
  the disc, and after that the drive answered nothing – and stayed so. What Lumen does when a disc does
  not open (ending the helper, the message, the restart) was checked with a stand-in for a library and
  helper that never return.
- Reading ahead works where the system has the disc mounted: always on macOS, drive letters on Windows,
  mounted discs on Linux. It does not reach disc images (`.iso`) and, on Linux, a drive read directly
  (`/dev/sr0`): libbluray reads those through libudfread, past its own file access. An image on a disk
  does not need it; a drive read directly on Linux still plays as in 1.4.0.
- **DVD** is unchanged: 0.4 s, no reading ahead.
- Windows: built and run through the tests on the build machines only.
- A drive that answers nothing even after the helper is gone has to be switched off and on. That happened
  once in three cases here.
- The macOS crash was not reproduced; the change closes the order of events its report shows.
- The helper processes Lumen ends are MakeMKV's (`makemkvcon guiserver`); those of MakeMKV's own window
  belong to a running program and are left alone.
