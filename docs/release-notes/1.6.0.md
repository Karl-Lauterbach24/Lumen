## New in LumenOS: a new look, sounds, a start picture

- **A start picture.** The system now shows Lumen's emblem with three breathing dots on the interface's
  dark field while it starts (plymouth); the interface opens with the same emblem in the same place,
  and the home screen's cards come in one after the other.
- **Motion throughout.** The selection in a list is a plate that glides from row to row, pages slide in
  and out, rows and cards arrive in turn, the selected card's glow breathes, the light in the
  background wanders slowly, and the emblem is cut free of its dark box.
- **Sounds and music.** Short sounds for moving, opening, going back, switches, errors and the start,
  and a quiet 72-second ambient loop in the menus. They are audio files (`resources/sounds`, rendered
  by `tools/make_sounds.sh` from `tools/soundgen`, so they are Lumen's own) and play on the output a
  film would use – and stay silent while a film has it. Your own files in `/etc/lumenos/sounds`
  replace them; an empty file silences one.
- **Screen saver.** After five minutes without input a clock drifts over black (an OLED television
  keeps the tiles in its picture otherwise); the key that wakes it does nothing else.
- *Settings › Interface* (sounds, music, their volumes, the screen saver). Translated into all
  16 languages. The start picture also works on machines with a serial console.

## New in LumenOS: HDR, the film's frame rate, Dolby and DTS as they are on the disc

Until now a film played inside the interface's window, and a window system stands between a film and
the display: the picture arrived in SDR, at the interface's refresh rate, and the sound decoded. Now,
on a machine with a graphics driver, the film has the display to itself while it runs
(*Settings › Picture and sound*):

- **HDR is passed through.** If the display takes HDR10 or HLG and the graphics driver can announce
  HDR to it, a film in HDR goes out as it is: 10 bits, its own curve, BT.2020, and the mastering data
  it carries. The display then does what it does with a disc player. A film whose curve the display
  does not take is converted – HLG to HDR10 where that is known, otherwise to SDR.
- **Dolby Vision and HDR10+** films play too, and arrive at the display as **HDR10**: mpv evaluates what
  they say about each scene. Their own signalling to the display travels in HDMI packets that Linux
  gives programs no way to send, and Dolby Vision needs Dolby's licensed processing in the player –
  so the display's "Dolby Vision" or "HDR10+" sign stays off. The nearest thing is a switch,
  *Update HDR data scene by scene (experimental)*, off unless you turn it on: the brightness these
  films name for each scene goes to the display as HDR10 data that changes from scene to scene. Some
  displays then treat each scene on its own; others take no notice or go dark for a moment at every
  change.
- **The film's frame rate.** The display is switched to a mode that fits – 23.976 Hz for a cinema film,
  50 or 59.94 for the rest – and back afterwards.
- **Dolby and DTS go to the receiver unchanged.** Lumen reads from the display's own description
  (EDID, which a receiver in front of it fills in) what the device decodes itself – Dolby Digital,
  Dolby Digital Plus with Atmos, Dolby TrueHD, DTS, DTS-HD – and sends exactly those as they are on
  the disc, over the HDMI output the display hangs on. Everything else is decoded and sent as PCM with
  as many channels as the device takes. If that output cannot be opened, the film plays with decoded
  sound through the system's output instead of staying silent.
- **A switch for each** of the three, and a list of sound outputs (a paired Bluetooth speaker appears
  there). *Output films straight to the display* turns the whole thing off: films then play in the
  window as in 1.5. Lumen does that by itself when the direct output cannot be opened on a machine,
  and when a driver keeps refusing the pictures it first tries without HDR, then the window.

The page is translated into all 16 languages. On a machine without a graphics driver (a virtual
machine) nothing changes: films play in the window.

Also in LumenOS: a switch in a list now shows its remark under its label (the date of the key
database was not visible before).

## MakeMKV on LumenOS, run for real

For 1.5.1 MakeMKV's setup had been checked only up to its licence. It has now been run to the end on a
test machine, with MakeMKV 2.0.0: built, installed, the beta key from its forum entered, and a test DVD
copied to an MKV file with the arguments Lumen's copy uses. Two things that run showed are put right:

- *Settings › Discs* named MakeMKV's version as "Use:" – `makemkvcon` has no switch for its version and
  answers with its help. The setup notes the version it installs.
- The compiler stayed on the device after the build (a packaging tool recommends it, which kept it),
  and so did 200 MB of downloaded packages. The setup now removes exactly what its build step added,
  except the libraries MakeMKV runs with, and the downloads.

## For developers

- **How a film gets the display.** `MpvController::setDirect()` (LumenOS only): mpv with
  `vo=gpu-next`, `gpu-context=drm`. Before a film is loaded, `lumenos-admin vt film` switches to a
  console of its own (8), which makes the compositor let go of the display; 450 ms after mpv is idle
  again, `vt ui` switches back. Lumen detaches from its controlling terminal in `--os` mode so that
  mpv's own console switching stays out of it. If Lumen ends while a film has the display, the next
  start of the session comes back from that console first.
- **Keys** while the film has the display: remotes as before (`InputMapper`); keyboards are read from
  evdev for that time (`InputMapper::setKeyboards`), because the compositor's input sleeps with it.
- **Frame rate.** A hook at `on_preloaded` reads the film's rate from the track list and sets
  `drm-mode`; `Kms::modeFor()` picks a whole multiple of the rate in the preferred resolution, 24
  frames once, everything else at 50 Hz or more, up to 0.11 % off (23.976 on 24.000).
- **HDR.** mpv's KMS output announces to the display the colour space of its render target, and it
  renders into that target; with OpenGL nothing sets the target by itself. So Lumen sets it per film,
  when the film's parameters are known: `target-trc`/`target-prim` to the film's curve and BT.2020 if
  the display takes it (`Edid`: the HDR and colorimetry blocks of the **first** CTA extension, as mpv
  reads them – more would produce a picture in HDR that is announced as SDR), otherwise `auto`. With
  an HDR target, `hdr-compute-peak` is off: measuring and adapting is the display's part.
  `target-colorspace-hint=yes` makes mpv send the film's own mastering data; the scene-by-scene switch
  sets `target-colorspace-hint-mode=source-dynamic`.
- **Sound.** `ao=alsa` on `alsa/hdmi:CARD=…,DEV=…` – the card whose ELD says a monitor is present –
  with `audio-spdif` from the EDID's audio blocks. PipeWire is left out while bitstreaming (it takes
  such a stream and plays it nowhere).
- `tools/build_deps.sh` builds **libdisplay-info** 0.2.0 as a static library into libmpv where the
  system has none – mpv's KMS output needs it – so the packages need nothing new on the system
  (libdrm, GBM and EGL were needed before). The media libraries are rebuilt once for this.
- In the log: `LumenOS: direkte Ausgabe HDMI-A-1 - Film pq/bt.2020 0.005-1000 cd/m2, CLL 1000,
  Bildschirm …` – one line per film, what was sent. `LUMEN_OS_DIRECT=0|1` in
  `/etc/lumenos/session.env` overrides the setting; `LUMEN_OS_EDID=<file>` and `LUMEN_OS_HDR=1` stand
  in for a display and a driver that are not there (how the page and the HDR path were tested).
- New test `tests/kms_test.cpp`: EDIDs of a television and of a receiver, and the choice of mode.

## Checked, and known limits

**None of this has been seen on a television or heard through a receiver.** There was no machine with a
graphics driver and an HDR display to try it on; what follows ran on an ARM64 virtual machine (QEMU with
KVM), with the direct output forced on its software renderer, a television's description put in its
place and an emulated sound card:

- a DVD image and films from a stick play straight to the virtual display, the interface comes back
  when they end or are stopped; pause, seeking and stop from the keyboard;
- the mode follows the film: 50 Hz for a PAL DVD and a 50-frame film, 60 Hz for 29.97 frames; a
  23.976-frame film stayed in the preferred mode because the virtual display has none that fits;
- an **HDR10** film and an **HLG** film go out in their own curve with BT.2020: colour bars arrive on
  the virtual display's picture within one step of the values in the file, so nothing is converted on
  the way; an SDR film stays SDR; with the switch off the HDR film is converted;
- a Dolby Digital track is sent as a bitstream when the (stand-in) television says it takes it; the
  emulated analogue card cannot open such a stream, and the film played with decoded sound instead;
- the fallback to the window when the direct output cannot be opened (met on the way: mpv's renderer
  refuses a software renderer unless told otherwise).

What that leaves open:

- **The sounds and the start picture were heard and seen only in a virtual machine** (the sounds were
  listened to by the author of the release, on files, not on a television's speakers). How long the
  screen is black between the start picture and the interface depends on the machine; in the virtual
  machine without a graphics driver it was about three seconds.
- **Whether a real display switches to HDR** – the connector's HDR properties, which only real graphics
  drivers have, were never written. The code that does it is mpv's.
- **Whether a receiver locks on to the bitstream**, and sound over HDMI at all.
- Switching the console away from the compositor and back on a real graphics driver, and how long a
  television takes to follow a change of mode (the picture is black for that time).
- Dolby Vision and HDR10+ files were not played, and the scene-by-scene switch was not seen at work.
- **Copying from a real drive.** MakeMKV does not accept the emulated drive of the test machine as one
  ("Failed to open disc" – shown as such on the copy page), so the copy that worked read the mounted
  disc's folder. Lumen's copy page hands MakeMKV the drive itself, as MakeMKV wants it for real discs.
- A display that names HDR only in a second extension block of its EDID gets SDR (mpv does not read it
  there).
- The colour depth on the cable is the driver's choice ("max bpc"); LumenOS renders 10 bits.
- S/PDIF outputs get decoded sound.
- Nvidia's own driver is not in the image; with the free one there is no HDR.

If the direct output misbehaves on a machine: *Settings › Picture and sound › Output films straight to
the display* off, and *Power › Restart Lumen only* – or `LUMEN_OS_DIRECT=0` in
`/etc/lumenos/session.env` from the maintenance console (Ctrl+Alt+F2).

Everything in the notes of [1.5.1](https://github.com/Karl-Lauterbach24/Lumen/releases/tag/v1.5.1)
about what was and was not checked of LumenOS still holds. Windows and macOS: nothing in this release
changes how they play.
